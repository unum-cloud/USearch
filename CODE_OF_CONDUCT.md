# Code of Conduct

## 1. The Ratatouille Principle

Great ideas and talent can come from anywhere, including unlikely individuals and AI agents.
We judge contributions by merit, not credentials or origin, but prefer accounts with traceable histories to simplify due diligence and build trust.

## 2. Think in Decades, Not in Pull Requests

Our projects represent years of engineering and commitments extending decades into the future.
Even excellent contributions may be rejected if they compromise architectural coherence or introduce complexity for marginal use cases.

Respect maintainer time and long-term priorities.
If you benefit from our work, consider contributing code, expertise, compute, or production experience.

## 3. Trace Ideas to Their Origins

We value original ideas and honest attribution, especially when commercializing open source.
Look beyond recent computer science papers and fashionable terminology: many supposedly novel techniques have much older roots in mathematics, physics, biology, or engineering.

Rebranding isn't invention.
Of course, after Apple put an "i" in front of everything, we're hardly innocent of putting a "U" in front of storage, search, and everything else.

## 4. Challenge Conventional Wisdom

> If you only do what you can do, you will never be more than you are now.
> — Master Shifu, Kung Fu Panda 3

Popularity is not proof of correctness.
Even widely accepted architectural principles, such as disaggregating storage and compute, deserve scrutiny against actual hardware, workloads, and engineering trade-offs.

Challenge our assumptions too.
Bring measurements, counterexamples, and better designs rather than appeals to authority or industry consensus.

## 5. Keep It About the Code

Discussions here are about the code.
Be direct and technical, assume good faith, and don't make it personal.
Maintainers moderate the issue tracker and pull requests at their discretion.
